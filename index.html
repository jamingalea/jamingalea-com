Jamin Galea

A London-based digital product designer. Currently, Lead Product Designer at Popsa focussed on memory-curation features on our native apps.

With over 15 years of multifaceted design experience, my work across web and native app interfaces isn’t just user-centred and visually refined; it tackles complexity and drives meaningful outcomes.


Originally from the small island of Malta, I’ve learned how limitations can spark ambition—pairing pragmatism with big-picture thinking.

Popsa — Web App

Popsa is an AI-powered platform helping millions of users curate, preserve and relive their memories through beautifully curated memories and printed photo products.

I was entrusted with starting Popsa’s web team. I helped plan, design and launch their first progressive web app in under 9 months. 12% of the revenue and 20% of new accounts now come from this platform. And basket-to-order conversion consistently exceeds the native apps by an average of 9.8% (Q2 ‘24-Q4 ‘25).

Arrival — CareHub

Arrival was an electric vehicle startup reimagining how vehicles are designed and manufactured. CareHub served as the central hub for all the information related to the vehicles and the business.

It was a comprehensive resource covering everything from financing and procurement to diagnostics and repairs. As it was accessible from every touchpoint, I focused heavily on creating a clear, intuitive content hierarchy. I scoped, planned and designed a year’s worth of features within 2 months, structured for release over 4 phases.

Soho House — House Pay

Soho House is a members club with more than 40 houses around the World.

'House Pay' was a first principles approach to a new payment system built into the members' app and integrated with a complex in-house POS systems. The new interface enhanced the members' experience and started the journey towards better data science for the business. It was also designed to improve the member experience, streamline operations and reduce fraudulent activities.

Arrival — Fleet Simulator

The fleet simulator allowed teams to remotely diagnose, monitor and manage virtual fleets through an interactive map and timeline.

While physical vehicles were still in production, the simulator provided realistic data across 200+ telemetry points, enabling teams to model real-world scenarios, test and iterate on code, validate internal procedures and build confidence in how the vehicles would behave once deployed.
